<b>Philemon</b><br><b>1</b> Paul, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, and Timothy our brother, unto Philemon our dearly beloved, and fellowlabourer, <b>2</b> And to our beloved Apphia, and Archippus our fellowsoldier, and to the church in thy house: <b>3</b> Grace to you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. <b>4</b> I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers, <b>5</b> Hearing of thy love and faith, which thou hast toward the Lord Jesus, and toward all saints; 
<b>6</b> That the communication of thy faith may become effectual by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus. <b>7</b> For we have great joy and consolation in thy love, because the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother. <b>8</b> Wherefore, though I might be much bold in Christ to enjoin thee that which is convenient, <b>9</b> Yet for love’s sake I rather beseech thee, being such an one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ. 
<b>10</b> I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds: <b>11</b> Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me: <b>12</b> Whom I have sent again: thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels: <b>13</b> Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel: <b>14</b> But without thy mind would I do nothing; that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly. 
<b>15</b> For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever; <b>16</b> Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord? <b>17</b> If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself. <b>18</b> If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account; <b>19</b> I Paul have written it with mine own hand, I will repay it: albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even thine own self besides. 
<b>20</b> Yea, brother, let me have joy of thee in the Lord: refresh my bowels in the Lord. <b>21</b> Having confidence in thy obedience I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say. <b>22</b> But withal prepare me also a lodging: for I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you. <b>23</b> There salute thee Epaphras, my fellowprisoner in Christ Jesus; <b>24</b> Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers. 
<b>25</b> The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen. Written from Rome to Philemon, by Onesimus a servant.
