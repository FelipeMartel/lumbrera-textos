<b>Proverbs 1</b><br><b>1</b> The parablis of Salomon, the sone of Dauid, king of Israel; <b>2</b> to kunne wisdom and kunnyng; <b>3</b> to vndurstonde the wordis of prudence; and to take the lernyng of teching; to take riytfulnesse, and dom, and equyte; <b>4</b> that felnesse be youun to litle children, and kunnyng, and vndurstonding to a yong wexynge man. <b>5</b> A wise man heringe schal be wisere; and a man vndurstondinge schal holde gouernails. 
<b>6</b> He schal perseyue a parable, and expownyng; the wordis of wise men, and the derk figuratif spechis of hem. <b>7</b> The drede of the Lord is the bigynning of wisdom; foolis dispisen wisdom and teching. <b>8</b> My sone, here thou the teching of thi fadir, and forsake thou not the lawe of thi modir; <b>9</b> that grace be addid, ethir encreessid, to thin heed, and a bie to thi necke. <b>10</b> Mi sone, if synneris flateren thee, assente thou not to hem. 
<b>11</b> If thei seien, Come thou with vs, sette we aspies to blood, hide we snaris of disseitis ayens an innocent without cause; <b>12</b> swolowe we him, as helle swolowith a man lyuynge; and al hool, as goynge doun in to a lake; we schulen fynde al preciouse catel, <b>13</b> we schulen fille oure housis with spuylis; sende thou lot with vs, <b>14</b> o purs be of vs alle; <b>15</b> my sone, go thou not with hem; forbede thi foot fro the pathis of hem. <b>16</b> For the feet of hem rennen to yuel; and thei hasten to schede out blood. 
<b>17</b> But a net is leid in veyn bifore the iyen of briddis, that han wengis. <b>18</b> Also `thilke wickid disseyueris setten aspies ayens her owne blood; and maken redi fraudis ayens her soulis. <b>19</b> So the pathis of ech auerouse man rauyschen the soulis of hem that welden. <b>20</b> Wisdom prechith with outforth; in stretis it yyueth his vois. <b>21</b> It crieth ofte in the heed of cumpenyes; in the leeues of yatis of the citee it bringith forth hise wordis, 
<b>22</b> and seith, Hou long, ye litle men in wit, louen yong childhod, and foolis schulen coueyte tho thingis, that ben harmful to hem silf, and vnprudent men schulen hate kunnyng? <b>23</b> Be ye conuertid at my repreuyng; lo, Y schal profre forth to you my spirit, and Y schal schewe my wordis. <b>24</b> For Y clepide, and ye forsoken; Y helde forth myn hond, and noon was that bihelde. <b>25</b> Ye dispisiden al my councel; and chargiden not my blamyngis. 
<b>26</b> And Y schal leiye in youre perisching; and Y schal scorne you, whanne that, that ye dreden, cometh to you. <b>27</b> Whanne sodeyne wretchidnesse fallith in, and perisching bifallith as tempest; whanne tribulacioun and angwisch cometh on you. <b>28</b> Thanne thei schulen clepe me, and Y schal not here; thei schulen rise eerli, and thei schulen not fynde me. <b>29</b> For thei hatiden teching, and thei token not the drede of the Lord, <b>30</b> nether assentiden to my councel, and depraueden al myn amendyng. 
<b>31</b> Therfor thei schulen ete the fruytis of her weie; and thei schulen be fillid with her counseils. <b>32</b> The turnyng awei of litle men in wit schal sle hem; and the prosperite of foolis schal leese hem. <b>33</b> But he that herith me, schal reste with outen drede; and he schal vse abundaunce, whanne the drede of yuels is takun awei. 
<b>Proverbs 2</b><br><b>1</b> Mi sone, if thou resseyuest my wordis, `and hidist myn heestis anentis thee; <b>2</b> that thin eere here wisdom, bowe thin herte to knowe prudence. <b>3</b> For if thou inwardli clepist wisdom, and bowist thin herte to prudence; <b>4</b> if thou sekist it as money, and diggist it out as tresours; <b>5</b> thanne thou schalt vndirstonde the drede of the Lord, and schalt fynde the kunnyng of God. <b>6</b> For the Lord yyueth wisdom; and prudence and kunnyng is of his mouth. 
<b>7</b> He schal kepe the heelthe of riytful men, and he schal defende hem that goen sympli. <b>8</b> And he schal kepe the pathis of riytfulnesse, and he schal kepe the weies of hooli men. <b>9</b> Thanne thou schalt vndirstonde riytfulnesse, and dom, and equytee, and ech good path. <b>10</b> If wysdom entrith in to thin herte, and kunnyng plesith thi soule, <b>11</b> good councel schal kepe thee, and prudence schal kepe thee; that thou be delyuered fro an yuel weie, <b>12</b> and fro a man that spekith weiward thingis. 
<b>13</b> Whiche forsaken a riytful weie, and goen bi derk weies; <b>14</b> whiche ben glad, whanne thei han do yuel, and maken ful out ioye in worste thingis; <b>15</b> whose weies ben weywerd, and her goyingis ben of yuel fame. <b>16</b> That thou be delyuered fro an alien womman, and fro a straunge womman, that makith soft hir wordis; <b>17</b> and forsakith the duyk of hir tyme of mariage, <b>18</b> and hath foryete the couenaunt of hir God. For the hous of hir is bowid to deeth, and hir pathis to helle. 
<b>19</b> Alle that entren to hir, schulen not turne ayen, nether schulen catche the pathis of lijf. <b>20</b> That thou go in a good weie, and kepe the pathis of iust men. <b>21</b> Forsothe thei that ben riytful, schulen dwelle in the lond; and symple men schulen perfitli dwelle ther ynne. <b>22</b> But vnfeithful men schulen be lost fro the loond; and thei that doen wickidli, schulen be takun awey fro it. 
<b>Proverbs 3</b><br><b>1</b> Mi sone, foryete thou not my lawe; and thyn herte kepe my comaundementis. <b>2</b> For tho schulen sette to thee the lengthe of daies, and the yeeris of lijf, and pees. <b>3</b> Merci and treuthe forsake thee not; bynde thou tho to thi throte, and write in the tablis of thin herte. <b>4</b> And thou schalt fynde grace, and good teching bifore God and men. <b>5</b> Haue thou trist in the Lord, of al thin herte; and triste thou not to thi prudence. 
<b>6</b> In alle thi weies thenke thou on hym, and he schal dresse thi goyngis. <b>7</b> Be thou not wijs anentis thi silf; drede thou God, and go awei fro yuel. <b>8</b> For whi helthe schal be in thi nawle, and moisting of thi boonys. <b>9</b> Onoure thou the Lord of thi catel, and of the beste of alle thi fruytis yyue thou to pore men; <b>10</b> and thi bernes schulen be fillid with abundaunce, and pressours schulen flowe with wiyn. <b>11</b> My sone, caste thou not awei the teching of the Lord; and faile thou not, whanne thou art chastisid of him. 
<b>12</b> For the Lord chastisith hym, whom he loueth; and as a fadir in the sone he plesith hym. <b>13</b> Blessid is the man that fyndith wisdom, and which flowith with prudence. <b>14</b> The geting therof is betere than the marchaundie of gold and of siluer; the fruytis therof ben the firste and clenneste. <b>15</b> It is preciousere than alle richessis; and alle thingis that ben desirid, moun not be comparisound to this. <b>16</b> Lengthe of daies is in the riythalf therof, and richessis and glorie ben in the lifthalf therof. 
<b>17</b> The weies therof ben feire weies, and alle the pathis therof ben pesible. <b>18</b> It is a tre of lijf to hem that taken it; and he that holdith it, is blessid. <b>19</b> The Lord foundide the erthe bi wisdom; he stablischide heuenes bi prudence. <b>20</b> The depthis of watris braken out bi his wisdom; and cloudis wexen togidere bi dewe. <b>21</b> My sone, these thingis flete not awey fro thin iyen; kepe thou my lawe, and my counsel; <b>22</b> and lijf schal be to thi soule, and grace `schal be to thi chekis. 
<b>23</b> Thanne thou schalt go tristili in thi weie; and thi foot schal not snapere. <b>24</b> If thou schalt slepe, thou schalt not drede; thou schalt reste, and thi sleep schal be soft. <b>25</b> Drede thou not bi sudeyne feer, and the powers of wickid men fallynge in on thee. <b>26</b> For the Lord schal be at thi side; and he schal kepe thi foot, that thou be not takun. <b>27</b> Nil thou forbede to do wel him that mai; if thou maist, and do thou wel. 
<b>28</b> Seie thou not to thi frend, Go, and turne thou ayen, and to morewe Y schal yyue to thee; whanne thou maist yyue anoon. <b>29</b> Ymagyne thou not yuel to thi freend, whanne he hath trist in thee. <b>30</b> Stryue thou not ayens a man with out cause, whanne he doith noon yuel to thee. <b>31</b> Sue thou not an vniust man, sue thou not hise weies. <b>32</b> For ech disseyuer is abhomynacioun of the Lord; and his speking is with simple men. 
<b>33</b> Nedinesse is sent of the Lord in the hous of a wickid man; but the dwelling places of iust men schulen be blessid. <b>34</b> He schal scorne scorneris; and he schal yyue grace to mylde men. <b>35</b> Wise men schulen haue glorie; enhaunsing of foolis is schenschipe. 
<b>Proverbs 4</b><br><b>1</b> Sones, here ye the teching of the fadir; and perseiue ye, that ye kunne prudence. <b>2</b> Y schal yyue to you a good yifte; forsake ye not my lawe. <b>3</b> For whi and Y was the sone of my fadir, a tendir sone, and oon `gendride bifore my modir. <b>4</b> And my fadir tauyte me, and seide, Thin herte resseyue my wordis; kepe thou myn heestis, and thou schalt lyue. <b>5</b> Welde thou wisdom, welde thou prudence; foryete thou not, nethir bowe thou awey fro the wordis of my mouth. 
<b>6</b> Forsake thou not it, and it schal kepe thee; loue thou it, and it schal kepe thee. <b>7</b> The bigynnyng of wisdom, welde thou wisdom; and in al thi possessioun gete thou prudence. <b>8</b> Take thou it, and it schal enhaunse thee; thou schalt be glorified of it, whanne thou hast biclippid it. <b>9</b> It schal yyue encresyngis of graces to thin heed; and a noble coroun schal defende thee. <b>10</b> Mi sone, here thou, and take my wordis; that the yeris of lijf be multiplied to thee. 
<b>11</b> Y schal schewe to thee the weie of wisdom; and Y schal lede thee bi the pathis of equyte. <b>12</b> In to whiche whanne thou hast entrid, thi goyngis schulen not be maad streit; and thou schalt rennen, and schalt not haue hirtyng. <b>13</b> Holde thou teching, and forsake it not; kepe thou it, for it is thi lijf. <b>14</b> Delite thou not in the pathis of wyckid men; and the weie of yuele men plese not thee. <b>15</b> Fle thou fro it, and passe thou not therbi; bowe thou awei, and forsake it. 
<b>16</b> For thei slepen not, `no but thei han do yuele; and sleep is rauyschid fro hem, no but thei han disseyued. <b>17</b> Thei eten the breed of vnpite, and drinken the wyn of wickidnesse. <b>18</b> But the path of iust men goith forth as liyt schynynge, and encreessith til to perfit dai. <b>19</b> The weie of wickid men is derk; thei witen not where thei schulen falle. <b>20</b> Mi sone, herkene thou my wordis; and bowe doun thin eeris to my spechis. <b>21</b> Go not tho awei fro thyn iyen; kepe thou hem in the myddil of thin herte. 
<b>22</b> For tho ben lijf to men fyndynge thoo, and heelthe `of al fleisch. <b>23</b> With al keping kepe thin herte, for lijf cometh forth of it. <b>24</b> Remoue thou a schrewid mouth fro thee; and backbitynge lippis be fer fro thee. <b>25</b> Thin iyen se riytful thingis; and thin iyeliddis go bifore thi steppis. <b>26</b> Dresse thou pathis to thi feet, and alle thi weies schulen be stablischid. 
<b>27</b> Bowe thou not to the riytside, nether to the leftside; turne awei thi foot fro yuel. For the Lord knowith the weies that ben at the riytside; but the weies ben weiward, that ben at the leftside. Forsothe he schal make thi goyngis riytful; and thi weies schulen be brouyt forth in pees. 
<b>Proverbs 5</b><br><b>1</b> Mi sone, perseyue thou my wisdom, and bowe doun thin eere to my prudence; that thou kepe thi thouytis, <b>2</b> and thi lippis kepe teching. Yyue thou not tent to the falsnesse of a womman; <b>3</b> for the lippis of an hoore ben an hony coomb droppinge, and hir throte is clerere than oile; <b>4</b> but the last thingis ben bittir as wormod, and hir tunge is scharp as a swerd keruynge on ech side. <b>5</b> Hir feet gon doun in to deeth; and hir steppis persen to hellis. 
<b>6</b> Tho goon not bi the path of lijf; hir steppis ben vncerteyn, and moun not be souyt out. <b>7</b> Now therfor, my sone, here thou me, and go not awei fro the wordis of my mouth. <b>8</b> Make fer thi weie fro hir, and neiye thou not to the doris of hir hous. <b>9</b> Yyue thou not thin onour to aliens, and thi yeeris to the cruel; <b>10</b> lest perauenture straungeris be fillid with thi strengthis, and lest thi trauels be in an alien hous; 
<b>11</b> and thou biweile in the laste daies, whanne thou hast wastid thi fleschis, and thi bodi; and thou seie, <b>12</b> Whi wlatide Y teching, and myn herte assentide not to blamyngis; <b>13</b> nether Y herde the voys of men techinge me, and Y bowide not doun myn eere to maistris? <b>14</b> Almest Y was in al yuel, in the myddis of the chirche, and of the synagoge. <b>15</b> Drinke thou watir of thi cisterne, and the floodis of thi pit. <b>16</b> Thi wellis be stremed forth; and departe thi watris in stretis. 
<b>17</b> Haue thou aloone `tho watris; and aliens be not thi parceneris. <b>18</b> Thi veyne be blessid; and be thou glad with the womman of thi yong wexynge age. <b>19</b> An hynde moost dereworthe; and an hert calf moost acceptable. Hir teetis fille thee in al tyme; and delite thou contynueli in the loue of hir. <b>20</b> Mi sone, whi art thou disseyued of an alien womman; and art fostrid in the bosum of an othere? <b>21</b> The Lord seeth the weie of a man; and biholdith alle hise steppis. 
<b>22</b> The wickidnessis of a wyckid man taken hym; and he is boundun with the roopis of hise synnes. <b>23</b> He schal die, for he hadde not lernyng; and he schal be disseyued in the mychilnesse of his fooli. 
<b>Proverbs 6</b><br><b>1</b> Mi sone, if thou hast bihiyt for thi freend; thou hast fastned thin hoond at a straunger. <b>2</b> Thou art boundun bi the wordis of thi mouth; and thou art takun with thin owne wordis. <b>3</b> Therfor, my sone, do thou that that Y seie, and delyuere thi silf; for thou hast fallun in to the hond of thi neiybore. Renne thou aboute, haste thou, reise thi freend; <b>4</b> yyue thou not sleep to thin iyen, nether thin iyeliddis nappe. 
<b>5</b> Be thou rauyschid as a doo fro the hond; and as a bridde fro aspiyngis of the foulere. <b>6</b> O! thou slowe man, go to the `amte, ether pissemyre; and biholde thou hise weies, and lerne thou wisdom. <b>7</b> Which whanne he hath no duyk, nethir comaundour, nether prince; <b>8</b> makith redi in somer mete to hym silf, and gaderith togidere in heruest that, that he schal ete. <b>9</b> Hou long schalt thou, slow man, slepe? whanne schalt thou rise fro thi sleep? 
<b>10</b> A litil thou schalt slepe, a litil thou schalt nappe; a litil thou schalt ioyne togidere thin hondis, that thou slepe. <b>11</b> And nedynesse, as a weigoere, schal come to thee; and pouert, as an armed man. Forsothe if thou art not slow, thi ripe corn schal come as a welle; and nedynesse schal fle fer fro thee. <b>12</b> A man apostata, a man vnprofitable, he goith with a weiward mouth; <b>13</b> he bekeneth with iyen, he trampith with the foot, he spekith with the fyngur, 
<b>14</b> bi schrewid herte he ymagyneth yuel, and in al tyme he sowith dissenciouns. <b>15</b> His perdicioun schal come to hym anoon, and he schal be brokun sodeynli; and he schal no more haue medecyn. <b>16</b> Sixe thingis ben, whyche the Lord hatith; and hise soule cursith the seuenthe thing. <b>17</b> Hiye iyen, a tunge liere, hondis schedinge out innocent blood, <b>18</b> an herte ymagynynge worste thouytis, feet swifte to renne in to yuel, <b>19</b> a man bringynge forth lesingis, a fals witnesse; and him that sowith discordis among britheren. 
<b>20</b> Mi sone, kepe the comaundementis of thi fadir; and forsake not the lawe of thi modir. <b>21</b> Bynde thou tho continueli in thin herte; and cumpasse `to thi throte. <b>22</b> Whanne thou goist, go tho with thee; whanne thou slepist, kepe tho thee; and thou wakynge speke with tho. <b>23</b> For the comaundement of God is a lanterne, and the lawe is liyt, and the blamyng of techyng is the weie of lijf; <b>24</b> `that the comaundementis kepe thee fro an yuel womman, and fro a flaterynge tunge of a straunge womman. 
<b>25</b> Thin herte coueite not the fairnesse of hir; nether be thou takun bi the signes of hir. <b>26</b> For the prijs of an hoore is vnnethe of o loof; but a womman takith the preciouse soule of a man. <b>27</b> Whether a man mai hide fier in his bosum, that hise clothis brenne not; <b>28</b> ethir go on colis, and hise feet be not brent? <b>29</b> So he that entrith to the wijf of his neiybore; schal not be cleene, whanne he hath touchid hir. <b>30</b> It is not greet synne, whanne a man stelith; for he stelith to fille an hungri soule. 
<b>31</b> And he takun schal yelde the seuenthe fold; and he schal yyue al the catel of his hous, and schal delyuere hym silf. <b>32</b> But he that is avouter; schal leese his soule, for the pouert of herte. <b>33</b> He gaderith filthe, and sclaundrith to hym silf; and his schenschip schal not be don awei. <b>34</b> For the feruent loue and strong veniaunce of the man schal not spare in the dai of veniaunce, <b>35</b> nether schal assente to the preieris of ony; nether schal take ful many yiftis for raunsum. 
<b>Proverbs 7</b><br><b>1</b> Mi sone, kepe thou my wordis; and kepe myn heestis to thee. Sone, onoure thou the Lord, and thou schalt be `myyti; but outakun hym drede thou not an alien. <b>2</b> Kepe thou myn heestis, and thou schalt lyue; and my lawe as the appil of thin iyen. <b>3</b> Bynde thou it in thi fyngris; write thou it in the tablis of thin herte. <b>4</b> Seie thou to wisdom, Thou art my sistir; and clepe thou prudence thi frendesse. <b>5</b> That it kepe thee fro a straunge womman; and fro an alien womman, that makith hir wordis swete. 
<b>6</b> For whi fro the wyndow of myn hous bi the latijs Y bihelde; and Y se litle children. <b>7</b> I biholde a yong man coward, <b>8</b> that passith bi the stretis, bisidis the corner; and he <b>9</b> goith niy the weie of hir hous in derk tyme, whanne the dai drawith to niyt, in the derknessis and myst of the nyyt. <b>10</b> And lo! a womman, maad redi with ournement of an hoore to disseyue soulis, meetith hym, and sche is a ianglere, and goynge about, <b>11</b> and vnpacient of reste, and mai not stonde in the hous with hir feet; 
<b>12</b> and now without forth, now in stretis, now bisidis corneris sche `aspieth. <b>13</b> And sche takith, and kissith the yong man; and flaterith with wowynge cheer, and seith, Y ouyte sacrifices for heelthe; <b>14</b> to dai Y haue yolde my vowis. <b>15</b> Therfor Y yede out in to thi meetyng, and Y desiride to se thee; and Y haue founde thee. <b>16</b> Y haue maad my bed with coordis, Y haue arayed with tapetis peyntid of Egipt; <b>17</b> Y haue bispreynt my bed with myrre, and aloes, and canel. 
<b>18</b> Come thou, be we fillid with tetis, and vse we collyngis that ben coueitid; til the dai bigynne to be cleer. <b>19</b> For myn hosebonde is not in his hows; he is goon a ful long weie. <b>20</b> He took with hym a bagge of money; he schal turne ayen in to his hous in the dai of ful moone. <b>21</b> Sche boonde hym with many wordis; and sche drow forth hym with flateryngis of lippis. <b>22</b> Anoon he as an oxe led to slayn sacrifice sueth hir, and as a ioli lomb and vnkunnynge; and the fool woot not, that he is drawun to bondys, 
<b>23</b> til an arowe perse his mawe. As if a brid hastith to the snare; and woot not, that it is don of the perel of his lijf. <b>24</b> Now therfor, my sone, here thou me; and perseyue the wordis of my mouth. <b>25</b> Lest thi soule be drawun awei in the weies of hir; nether be thou disseyued in the pathis of hir. <b>26</b> For sche castide doun many woundid men; and alle strongeste men weren slayn of hir. <b>27</b> The weies of helle is hir hous; and persen in to ynnere thingis of deeth. 
<b>Proverbs 8</b><br><b>1</b> Whether wisdom crieth not ofte; and prudence yyueth his vois? <b>2</b> In souereyneste and hiy coppis, aboue the weie, in the myddis of pathis, <b>3</b> and it stondith bisidis the yate of the citee, in thilke closyngis, and spekith, and seith, A! <b>4</b> ye men, Y crie ofte to you; and my vois is to the sones of men. <b>5</b> Litle children, vndirstonde ye wisdom; and ye vnwise men, `perseyue wisdom. <b>6</b> Here ye, for Y schal speke of grete thingis; and my lippis schulen be openyd, to preche riytful thingis. 
<b>7</b> My throte schal bithenke treuthe; and my lippis schulen curse a wickid man. <b>8</b> My wordis ben iust; no schrewid thing, nether weiward is in tho. <b>9</b> `My wordis ben riytful to hem that vndurstonden; and ben euene to hem that fynden kunnyng. <b>10</b> Take ye my chastisyng, and not money; chese ye teching more than tresour. <b>11</b> For wisdom is betere than alle richessis moost preciouse; and al desirable thing mai not be comparisound therto. <b>12</b> Y, wisdom, dwelle in counsel; and Y am among lernyd thouytis. 
<b>13</b> The drede of the Lord hatith yuel; Y curse boost, and pride, and a schrewid weie, and a double tungid mouth. <b>14</b> Counseil is myn, and equyte `is myn; prudence is myn, and strengthe `is myn. <b>15</b> Kyngis regnen bi me; and the makeris of lawis demen iust thingis bi me. <b>16</b> Princis comaunden bi me; and myyti men demen riytfulnesse bi me. <b>17</b> I loue hem that louen me; and thei that waken eerli to me, schulen fynde me. <b>18</b> With me ben rychessis, and glorie; souereyn richessis, and riytfulnesse.

<b>10</b> He that disseyueth iust men in an yuel weye, schal falle in his perisching; and iuste men schulen welde hise goodis. <b>11</b> A ryche man semeth wijs to him silf; but a pore man prudent schal serche him. <b>12</b> In enhaunsing of iust men is miche glorie; whanne wickid men regnen, fallyngis of men ben. <b>13</b> He that hidith hise grete trespassis, schal not be maad riytful; but he that knoulechith and forsakith tho, schal gete merci. 
<b>14</b> Blessid is the man, which is euere dredeful; but he that is `harde of soule, schal falle in to yuel. <b>15</b> A rorynge lioun, and an hungry bere, is a wickid prince on a pore puple. <b>16</b> A duyk nedi of prudence schal oppresse many men bi fals chalenge; but the daies of hym that hatith aueryce, schulen be maad longe. <b>17</b> No man susteyneth a man that falsly chalengith the blood of a man, if he fleeth `til to the lake. <b>18</b> He that goith simpli, schal be saaf; he that goith bi weiward weies, schal falle doun onys. 
<b>19</b> He that worchith his lond, schal be fillid with looues; he that sueth ydelnesse, schal be fillid with nedynesse. <b>20</b> A feithful man schal be preisid myche; but he that hastith to be maad riche, schal not be innocent. <b>21</b> He that knowith a face in doom, doith not wel; this man forsakith treuthe, yhe, for a mussel of breed. <b>22</b> A man that hastith to be maad riche, and hath enuye to othere men; woot not that nedinesse schal come on hym. 
<b>23</b> He that repreueth a man, schal fynde grace aftirward at hym; more than he that disseyueth bi flateryngis of tunge. <b>24</b> He that withdrawith ony thing fro his fadir and fro his modir, and seith that this is no synne, is parcener of a manquellere. <b>25</b> He that auauntith hym silf, and alargith, reisith stryues; but he that hopith in the Lord, schal be sauyd. <b>26</b> He that tristith in his herte, is a fool; but he that goith wiseli, 
<b>27</b> schal be preysid. He that yyueth to a pore man, schal not be nedi; he that dispisith `a pore man bisechynge, schal suffre nedynesse. <b>28</b> Whanne vnpitouse men risen, men schulen be hid; whanne tho `vnpitouse men han perischid, iust men schulen be multiplied. 
<b>Proverbs 29</b><br><b>1</b> Sodeyn perischyng schal come on that man, that with hard nol dispisith a blamere; and helth schal not sue hym. <b>2</b> The comynalte schal be glad in the multipliyng of iust men; whanne wickid men han take prinshod, the puple schal weyle. <b>3</b> A man that loueth wisdom, makith glad his fadir; but he that nurschith `an hoore, schal leese catel. <b>4</b> A iust king reisith the lond; an auerouse man schal distrie it. 
<b>5</b> A man that spekith bi flaterynge and feyned wordis to his frend; spredith abrood a net to hise steppis. <b>6</b> A snare schal wlappe a wickid man doynge synne; and a iust man schal preise, and schal make ioye. <b>7</b> A iust man knowith the cause of pore men; an vnpitouse man knowith not kunnyng. <b>8</b> Men ful of pestilence distryen a citee; but wise men turnen awei woodnesse. <b>9</b> If a wijs man stryueth with a fool; whether he be wrooth, `ether he leiyith, he schal not fynde reste. 
<b>10</b> Menquelleris haten a simple man; but iust men seken his soule. <b>11</b> A fool bringith forth al his spirit; a wise man dilaieth, and reserueth in to tyme comynge afterward. <b>12</b> A prince that herith wilfuli the wordis of a leesyng; schal haue alle mynystris vnfeithful. <b>13</b> A pore man and a leenere metten hem silf; the Lord is liytnere of euer ethir. <b>14</b> If a kyng demeth pore men in treuthe; his trone schal be maad stidfast with outen ende. 
<b>15</b> A yerde and chastisyng schal yyue wisdom; but a child, which is left to his wille, schendith his modir. <b>16</b> Grete trespassis schulen be multiplied in the multipliyng of wickid men; and iust men schulen se the fallyngis of hem. <b>17</b> Teche thi sone, and he schal coumforte thee; and he schal yyue delicis to thi soule. <b>18</b> Whanne prophesie faylith, the puple schal be distried; but he that kepith the lawe, is blessid. 
<b>19</b> A seruaunt mai not be tauyt bi wordis; for he vndirstondith that that thou seist, and dispisith for to answere. <b>20</b> Thou hast seyn a man swift to speke; foli schal be hopid more than his amendyng. <b>21</b> He that nurschith his seruaunt delicatli fro childhod; schal fynde hym rebel aftirward. <b>22</b> A wrathful man territh chidingis; and he that is liyt to haue indignacioun, schal be more enclynaunt to synnes. <b>23</b> Lownesse sueth a proude man; and glorie schal vp take a meke man of spirit. 
<b>24</b> He that takith part with a theef, hatith his soule; he herith a man chargynge greetli, and schewith not. <b>25</b> He that dredith a man, schal falle soon; he that hopith in the Lord, shal be reisid. <b>26</b> Many men seken the face of the prince; and the doom of alle men schal go forth of the Lord. <b>27</b> Iust men han abhomynacioun of a wickid man; and wickid men han abhomynacioun of hem, that ben in a riytful weye. A sone kepynge a word, schal be out of perdicioun. 
<b>Proverbs 30</b><br><b>1</b> The wordis of hym that gaderith, of the sone spuynge. The prophesie which a man spak, with whom God was, and which man was coumfortid bi God dwellyng with hym, <b>2</b> and seide, Y am the moost fool of men; and the wisdom of men is not with me. <b>3</b> Y lernede not wisdom; and Y knew not the kunnyng of hooli men. 
<b>4</b> Who stiede in to heuene, and cam doun? Who helde togidere the spirit in hise hondis? who bonde togidere watris as in a cloth? Who reiside alle the endis of erthe? What is name of hym? and what is the name of his sone, if thou knowist? <b>5</b> Ech word of God is a scheld set a fiere, to alle that hopen in hym. <b>6</b> Adde thou not ony thing to the wordis of hym, and thou be repreued, and be foundun a liere. <b>7</b> I preiede thee twei thingis; denye not thou to me, bifor that Y die. 
<b>8</b> Make thou fer fro me vanyte and wordis of leesyng; yyue thou not to me beggery and richessis; yyue thou oneli necessaries to my lijflode; <b>9</b> lest perauenture Y be fillid, and be drawun to denye, and seie, Who is the Lord? and lest Y compellid bi nedynesse, stele, and forswere the name of my God. <b>10</b> Accuse thou not a seruaunt to his lord, lest perauenture he curse thee, and thou falle doun. <b>11</b> A generacioun that cursith his fadir, and that blessith not his modir. 
<b>12</b> A generacioun that semeth cleene to it silf, and netheles is not waischun fro hise filthis. <b>13</b> A generacioun whose iyen ben hiy, and the iye liddis therof ben reisid in to hiy thingis. <b>14</b> A generacioun that hath swerdis for teeth, and etith with hise wank teeth; that it ete nedi men of erthe, and the porails of men. <b>15</b> The watir leche hath twei douytris, seiynge, Brynge, bringe. Thre thingis ben vnable to be fillid, and the fourthe, that seith neuere, It suffisith; 
<b>16</b> helle, and the mouth of the wombe, and the erthe which is neuere fillid with water; but fier seith neuere, It suffisith. <b>17</b> Crowis of the stronde picke out thilke iye, that scorneth the fadir, and that dispisith the child beryng of his modir; and the briddis of an egle ete that iye. <b>18</b> Thre thingis ben hard to me, and outirli Y knowe not the fourthe thing; <b>19</b> the weye of an egle in heuene, the weie of a serpent on a stoon, the weie of a schip in the myddil of the see, and the weie of a man in yong wexynge age. 
<b>20</b> Siche is the weie of a womman auowtresse, which etith, and wipith hir mouth, and seith, Y wrouyte not yuel. <b>21</b> The erthe is moued bi thre thingis, and the fourthe thing, which it may not susteyne; <b>22</b> bi a seruaunt, whanne he regneth; bi a fool, whanne he is fillid with mete; <b>23</b> bi an hateful womman, whanne sche is takun in matrymonye; and bi an handmaide, whanne sche is eir of hir ladi. <b>24</b> Foure ben the leeste thingis of erthe, and tho ben wisere than wise men; 
<b>25</b> amtis, a feble puple, that maken redi mete in heruest to hem silf; <b>26</b> a hare, a puple vnmyyti, that settith his bed in a stoon; <b>27</b> a locust hath no kyng, and al goith out bi cumpanyes; an euete enforsith with hondis, <b>28</b> and dwellith in the housis of kingis. <b>29</b> Thre thingis ben, that goon wel, and the fourthe thing, that goith richeli. <b>30</b> A lioun, strongeste of beestis, schal not drede at the meetyng of ony man; <b>31</b> a cok gird the leendis, and a ram, and noon is that schal ayenstonde him. 
<b>32</b> He that apperith a fool, aftir that he is reisid an hiy; for if he hadde vndurstonde, he hadde sett hond on his mouth. <b>33</b> Forsothe he that thristith strongli teetis, to drawe out mylk, thristith out botere; and he that smytith greetli, drawith out blood; and he that stirith iris, bringith forth discordis. 
<b>Proverbs 31</b><br><b>1</b> The wordis of Lamuel, the king; the visioun bi which his modir tauyte hym. <b>2</b> What my derlyng? what the derlyng of my wombe? what the derlyng of my desiris? <b>3</b> Yyue thou not thi catel to wymmen, and thi richessis to do awei kyngis. <b>4</b> A! Lamuel, nyle thou yiue wyn to kingis; for no pryuete is, where drunkenesse regneth. <b>5</b> Lest perauenture thei drynke, and foryete domes, and chaunge the cause of the sones of a pore man. 
<b>6</b> Yyue ye sidur to hem that morenen, and wyn to hem that ben of bitter soule. <b>7</b> Drinke thei, and foryete thei her nedinesse; and thenke thei no more on her sorewe. <b>8</b> Opene thi mouth for a doumb man, <b>9</b> and opene thi mouth for the causes of alle sones that passen forth. Deme thou that that is iust, and deme thou a nedi man and a pore man. <b>10</b> Who schal fynde a stronge womman? the prijs of her is fer, and fro the laste endis. <b>11</b> The herte of hir hosebond tristith in hir; and sche schal not haue nede to spuylis. 
<b>12</b> Sche schal yelde to hym good, and not yuel, in alle the daies of hir lijf. <b>13</b> Sche souyte wolle and flex; and wrouyte bi the counsel of hir hondis. <b>14</b> Sche is maad as the schip of a marchaunt, that berith his breed fro fer. <b>15</b> And sche roos bi nyyt, and yaf prey to hir meyneals, and metis to hir handmaidis. <b>16</b> Sche bihelde a feeld, and bouyte it; of the fruyt of hir hondis sche plauntide a vyner. <b>17</b> Sche girde hir leendis with strengthe, and made strong hir arm. 
<b>18</b> Sche taastide, and siy, that hir marchaundie was good; hir lanterne schal not be quenchid in the niyt. <b>19</b> Sche putte hir hondis to stronge thingis, and hir fyngris token the spyndil. <b>20</b> Sche openyde hir hond to a nedi man, and stretchide forth hir hondis to a pore man. <b>21</b> Sche schal not drede for hir hous of the cooldis of snow; for alle hir meyneals ben clothid with double clothis. <b>22</b> Sche made to hir a ray cloth; bijs and purpur is the cloth of hir. 
<b>23</b> Hir hosebonde is noble in the yatis, whanne he sittith with the senatours of erthe. <b>24</b> Sche made lynnun cloth, and selde; and yaf a girdil to a Chananei. <b>25</b> Strengthe and fairnesse is the clothing of hir; and sche schal leiye in the laste dai. <b>26</b> Sche openyde hir mouth to wisdom; and the lawe of merci is in hir tunge. <b>27</b> Sche bihelde the pathis of hir hous; and sche eet not breed idili. <b>28</b> Hir sones risiden, and prechiden hir moost blessid; hir hosebonde roos, and preiside hir. 
<b>29</b> Many douytris gaderiden richessis; thou passidist alle. <b>30</b> Fairnesse is disseiuable grace, and veyn; thilke womman, that dredith the Lord, schal be preisid. <b>31</b> Yyue ye to hir of the fruyt of hir hondis; and hir werkis preise hir in the yatis.
