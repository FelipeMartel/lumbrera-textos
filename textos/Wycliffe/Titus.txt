<b>Titus 1</b><br><b>1</b> Poul, the seruaunt of God, and apostle of Jhesu Crist, bi the feith of the chosun of God, and bi the knowing of the treuthe, <b>2</b> whiche is aftir pitee, in to the hope of euerlastinge lijf, which lijf God that lieth not, bihiyte bifore tymes of the world; <b>3</b> but he hath schewid in hise tymes his word in preching, that is bitakun to me bi the comaundement of `God oure sauyour, <b>4</b> to Tite, most dereworthe sone bi the comyn feith, grace and pees of God the fadir, and of Crist Jhesu, oure sauyour. 
<b>5</b> For cause of this thing Y lefte thee at Crete, that thou amende tho thingis that failen, and ordeyne preestis bi citees, as also Y disposide to thee. <b>6</b> If ony man is withoute cryme, an hosebonde of o wijf, and hath feithful sones, not in accusacioun of letcherie, or not suget. <b>7</b> For it bihoueth a bischop to be without cryme, a dispendour of God, not proud, not wrathful, not drunkelew, not smytere, not coueytouse of foul wynnyng; <b>8</b> but holdinge hospitalite, benygne, prudent, sobre, iust, 
<b>9</b> hooli, contynent, takinge that trewe word, that is aftir doctryn; that he be miyti to amoneste in hoolsum techyng, and to repreue hem that ayenseien. <b>10</b> For ther ben many vnobedient, and veyn spekeris, and disseyueris, moost thei that ben of circumcisyoun, <b>11</b> whiche it bihoueth to be repreued; whiche subuerten alle housis, techinge whiche thingis it bihoueth not, for the loue of foul wynnyng. <b>12</b> And oon of hem, her propre profete, seide, Men of Crete ben euere more lyeris, yuele beestis, of slowe wombe. 
<b>13</b> This witnessyng is trewe. For what cause blame hem sore, that thei be hool in feith, <b>14</b> not yyuynge tent to fablis of Jewis, and to maundementis of men, that turnen awei hem fro treuthe. <b>15</b> And alle thingis ben clene to clene men; but to vnclene men and to vnfeithful no thing is clene, for the soule and conscience of hem ben maad vnclene. <b>16</b> Thei knoulechen that thei knowen God, but bi dedis thei denyen; whanne thei ben abhominable, and vnbileueful, and repreuable to al good werk. 
<b>Titus 2</b><br><b>1</b> But speke thou tho thingis that bisemen hoolsum teching; <b>2</b> that elde men be sobre, chast, prudent, hool in feith, in loue, and pacience; <b>3</b> also olde wymmen in hooli abite, not sclaundereris, not seruynge myche to wyn, wel techynge, that thei teche prudence. <b>4</b> Moneste thou yonge wymmen, that thei loue here hosebondis, that thei loue her children; <b>5</b> and that thei be prudent, chast, sobre, hauynge cure of the hous, benygne, suget to her hosebondis, that the word of God be not blasfemyd. 
<b>6</b> Also moneste thou yonge men, that thei be sobre. <b>7</b> In alle thingis yyue thi silf ensaumple of good werkis, in teching, in hoolnesse, in sadnesse. <b>8</b> An hoolsum word, and vnrepreuable; that he that is of the contrarie side, be aschamed, hauynge noon yuel thing to seie of you. <b>9</b> Moneste thou seruauntis to be suget to her lordis; in alle thingis plesinge, not ayenseiynge, not defraudynge, <b>10</b> but in alle thingis schewinge good feith, that thei onoure in alle thingis the doctryn of `God, oure sauyour. 
<b>11</b> For the grace of `God, oure sauyour, <b>12</b> hath apperid to alle men, and tauyte vs, that we forsake wickidnesse, and worldli desyris, lyue sobreli, and iustli, `and piteuousli in this world, <b>13</b> abidinge the blessid hope and the comyng of the glorie of the greet God, and of oure sauyour Jhesu Crist; <b>14</b> that yaf hym silf for vs, to ayenbie vs fro al wickidnesse, and make clene to hym silf a puple acceptable, and suere of good werkis. 
<b>15</b> Speke thou these thingis, and moneste thou, and repreue thou with al comaundement; no man dispise thee. 
<b>Titus 3</b><br><b>1</b> Amoneste hem to be sugetis to prynces, and to poweris; to obeische to that that is seid, and to be redi to al good werk; <b>2</b> to blasfeme no man, to be not ful of chiding, but temperat, schewynge al myldenesse to alle men. <b>3</b> For we weren sum tyme vnwise, vnbileueful, errynge, and seruynge to desiris, and to dyuerse lustis, doynge in malice and enuye, worthi to be hatid, hatinge ech othere. <b>4</b> But whanne the benygnyte and the manhed of oure sauyour God aperide, 
<b>5</b> not of werkis of riytwisnesse that we diden, but bi his merci he made vs saaf, bi waischyng of ayen bigetyng, and ayen newyng of the Hooli Goost, <b>6</b> whom he schedde into vs plenteuousli bi Jhesu Crist, <b>7</b> oure saueour, that we iustified bi his grace, ben eiris by hope of euerlastinge lijf. <b>8</b> A trewe word is, and of these thingis Y wole that thou conferme othere, that thei that bileuen to God, be bisy to be aboue othere in good werkis. These thingis ben good, and profitable to men. 
<b>9</b> And eschewe thou foltische questiouns, and genologies, and stryues, and fiytyngis of the lawe; for tho ben vnprofitable and veyn. <b>10</b> Eschewe thou a man eretik, aftir oon and the secound correccioun; <b>11</b> witinge that he that is siche a maner man is subuertid, and trespassith, and is dampned bi his owne dom. <b>12</b> Whanne Y sende to thee Arteman, or Titicus, hiy thou to `come to me to Nycopolis; for Y haue purposid to dwelle in wyntir there. 
<b>13</b> Bisili byfor sende Zenam, a wise man of lawe, and Apollo, that no thing faile to hem. <b>14</b> Thei that ben of ouris, lerne to be gouernouris in good werkis, to necessarie vsis, that thei be not with out fruyt. <b>15</b> Alle men that ben with me greeten thee wel. Grete thou wel hem, that louen vs in feith. The grace of God be with you alle. Amen.
