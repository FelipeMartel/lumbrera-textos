<b>Job 1</b><br><b>1</b> `A man, Joob bi name, was in the lond of Hus; and thilke man was symple, and riytful, and dredynge God, and goynge awey fro yuel. <b>2</b> And seuene sones and thre douytris weren borun to hym; <b>3</b> and his possessioun was seuene thousynde of scheep, and thre thousynde of camels, and fyue hundrid yockis of oxis, and fyue hundrid of femal assis, and ful myche meynee; and `thilke man was grete among alle men of the eest. 
<b>4</b> And hise sones yeden, and maden feestis bi housis, ech man in his day; and thei senten, and clepiden her thre sistris, `that thei schulden ete, and drynke wiyn with hem. <b>5</b> And whanne the daies of feeste hadden passid in to the world, Joob sente to hem, and halewide hem, and he roos eerli, and offride brent sacrifices `bi alle. For he seide, Lest perauenture my sones do synne, and curse God in her hertis. Joob dide so in alle daies. 
<b>6</b> Forsothe in sum day, whanne the sones of God `weren comun to be present bifor the Lord, also Sathan cam among hem. <b>7</b> To whom the Lord seide, Fro whennus comest thou? Which answeride, and seide, Y haue cumpassid the erthe, and Y haue walkid thorouy it. <b>8</b> And the Lord seide to hym, Whether thou hast biholde my seruaunt Joob, that noon in erthe is lyik hym; he is a symple man, and riytful, and dredynge God, and goynge awei fro yuel? <b>9</b> To whom Sathan answeride, Whether Joob dredith God veynli? 
<b>10</b> Whethir thou hast not cumpassid hym, and his hows, and al his catel bi cumpas? Thou hast blessid the werkis of hise hondis, and hise possessioun encreesside in erthe. <b>11</b> But stretche forth thin hond a litil, and touche thou alle thingis whiche he hath in possessioun; if he cursith not thee `in the face, `bileue not to me. <b>12</b> Therfor the Lord seide to Sathan, Lo! alle thingis, whiche he hath, ben in thin hond; oneli stretche thou not forth thin hond in to hym. And Sathan yede out fro the face of the Lord. 
<b>13</b> Sotheli whanne in sum dai `hise sones and douytris eeten, and drunken wiyn in the hows of her firste gendrid brothir, <b>14</b> a messanger cam to Job, `whiche messanger seide, Oxis eriden, and femal assis `weren lesewid bisidis tho; <b>15</b> and Sabeis felden yn, and token awey alle thingis, and `smytiden the children with swerd; and Y aloone ascapide for to telle to thee. 
<b>16</b> And whanne he spak yit, anothir cam, and seide, Fier of God cam doun fro heuene, and wastide scheep, and `children touchid; and Y aloone ascapide for to telle `to thee. <b>17</b> But yit the while he spak, also anothir cam, and seide, Caldeis maden thre cumpenyes, and assailiden the camels, and token tho awei, and thei smytiden `also the children with swerd; and Y aloone ascapide to telle to thee. 
<b>18</b> And yit he spak, and, lo! anothir entride, and seide, While thi sones and douytris eeten, and drunken wiyn in the hows of her firste gendrid brothir, <b>19</b> a greet wynde felde yn sudenli fro the coost of desert, and schook foure corneris of the hows, `which felde doun, and oppresside thi children, and thei ben deed; and Y aloone fledde to telle to thee. <b>20</b> Thanne Joob roos, and to-rente hise clothis, and `with pollid heed he felde doun on the erthe, and worschipide God, 
<b>21</b> and seide, Y yede nakid out of the wombe of my modir, Y schal turne ayen nakid thidur; the Lord yaf, the Lord took awei; as it pleside the Lord, so `it is doon; the name of the Lord be blessid. <b>22</b> In alle these thingis Joob synnede not in hise lippis, nether spak ony fonned thing ayens God. 
<b>Job 2</b><br><b>1</b> Forsothe it was doon, whanne in sum dai the sones of God `weren comun, and stoden bifor the Lord, and Sathan `was comun among hem, and stood in his siyt, <b>2</b> that the Lord seide to Sathan, Fro whennus comest thou? Which answeride, and seide, Y haue cumpassid the erthe, `and Y haue go thury it. 
<b>3</b> And the Lord seide to Sathan, Whethir thou hast biholde my seruaunt Joob, that noon in erthe is lijk hym; he is a symple man, and riytful, and dredynge God, and goynge awei fro yuel, and yit holdynge innocence? `But thou hast moued me ayens him, that `Y schulde turmente hym in veyn. <b>4</b> To whom Sathan answeride, and seide, `A man schal yyue skyn for skyn, and alle thingis that he hath for his lijf; <b>5</b> `ellis sende thin hond, and touche his boon and fleisch, and thanne thou schalt se, that he schal curse thee in the face. 
<b>6</b> Therfor the Lord seide to Sathan, Lo! he is in `thin hond; netheles kepe thou his lijf. <b>7</b> Therfor Sathan yede out fro the face of the Lord, and smoot Joob with `a ful wickid botche fro the sole of the foot `til to his top; <b>8</b> which Joob schauyde the quytere with a schelle, `and sat in the dunghil. <b>9</b> Forsothe his wijf seide to hym, Dwellist thou yit in thi symplenesse? Curse thou God, and die. 
<b>10</b> And Joob seide, Thou hast spoke as oon of the fonned wymmen; if we han take goodis of the hond of the Lord, whi forsothe suffren we not yuels? In alle these thingis Joob synnede not in hise lippis. <b>11</b> Therfor thre frendis of Joob herden al the yuel, that hadde bifelde to hym, and camen ech man fro his place, Eliphath Temanytes, and Baldach Suythes, and Sophar Naamathites; for thei `hadden seide togidere to hem silf, that thei wolden come togidere, and visite hym, and coumforte. 
<b>12</b> And whanne thei hadden reisid afer `her iyen, thei knewen not hym; and thei crieden, and wepten, and to-renten her clothis, and spreynten dust on her heed `in to heuene. <b>13</b> And thei saten with hym in the erthe seuene daies and seuene nyytis, and no man spak a word to hym; for thei sien, that his sorewe was greet. 
<b>Job 3</b><br><b>1</b> Aftir these thingis Joob openyde his mouth, <b>2</b> and curside his dai, and seide, Perische the dai in which Y was borun, <b>3</b> and the nyyt in which it was seid, The man is conceyued. <b>4</b> Thilke dai be turnede in to derknessis; God seke not it aboue, and be it not in mynde, nethir be it liytned with liyt. <b>5</b> Derknessis make it derk, and the schadewe of deeth and myist occupie it; and be it wlappid with bittirnesse. 
<b>6</b> Derk whirlwynde holde that niyt; be it not rikynyd among the daies of the yeer, nethir be it noumbrid among the monethes. <b>7</b> Thilke nyyt be soleyn, and not worthi of preisyng. <b>8</b> Curse thei it, that cursen the dai, that ben redi to reise Leuyathan. <b>9</b> Sterris be maad derk with the derknesse therof; abide it liyt, and se it not, nethir the bigynnyng of the morwetid risyng vp. <b>10</b> For it closide not the doris of the wombe, that bar me, nethir took awei yuels fro min iyen. 
<b>11</b> Whi was not Y deed in the wombe? whi yede Y out of the wombe, and perischide not anoon? <b>12</b> Whi was Y takun on knees? whi was Y suclid with teetis? <b>13</b> For now Y slepynge schulde be stille, and schulde reste in my sleep, <b>14</b> with kyngis, and consuls of erthe, that bilden to hem soleyn places; <b>15</b> ethir with prynces that han gold in possessioun, and fillen her housis with siluer; <b>16</b> ethir as a `thing hid not borun Y schulde not stonde, ethir whiche conseyued sien not liyt. 
<b>17</b> There wickid men ceessiden of noise, and there men maad wery of strengthe restiden. <b>18</b> And sum tyme boundun togidere with out disese thei herden not the voys of the wrongful axere. <b>19</b> A litil man and greet man be there, and a seruaunt free fro his lord. <b>20</b> Whi is liyt youun to the wretche, and lijf to hem that ben in bitternesse of soule? <b>21</b> Whiche abiden deeth, and it cometh not; <b>22</b> as men diggynge out tresour and ioien greetly, whanne thei han founde a sepulcre? 
<b>23</b> Whi is liyt youun to a man, whos weie is hid, and God hath cumpassid hym with derknessis? <b>24</b> Bifore that Y ete, Y siyhe; and as of watir flowynge, so is my roryng. <b>25</b> For the drede, which Y dredde, cam to me; and that, that Y schamede, bifelde. <b>26</b> Whether Y dissymilide not? whether Y was not stille? whether Y restide not? and indignacioun cometh on me. 
<b>Job 4</b><br><b>1</b> Forsothe Eliphat Themanytes answeride, and seide, <b>2</b> If we bigynnen to speke to thee, in hap thou schalt take it heuyli; but who may holde a word conseyued? <b>3</b> Lo! thou hast tauyt ful many men, and thou hast strengthid hondis maad feynt. <b>4</b> Thi wordis confermyden men doutynge, and thou coumfortidist knees tremblynge. <b>5</b> But now a wounde is comun on thee, and thou hast failid; it touchide thee, and thou art disturblid. 
<b>6</b> Where is thi drede, thi strengthe, and thi pacience, and the perfeccioun of thi weies? <b>7</b> Y biseche thee, haue thou mynde, what innocent man perischide euere, ethir whanne riytful men weren doon awei? <b>8</b> Certis rathir Y siy hem, that worchen wickidnesse, and sowen sorewis, <b>9</b> and repen tho, to haue perischid bi God blowynge, and to be wastid bi the spirit of his ire. <b>10</b> The roryng of a lioun, and the vois of a lionesse, and the teeth of `whelpis of liouns ben al to-brokun. 
<b>11</b> Tigris perischide, for sche hadde not prey; and the whelpis of a lioun ben distried. <b>12</b> Certis an hid word was seid to me, and myn eere took as theueli the veynes of priuy noise therof. <b>13</b> In the hidousnesse of `nyytis siyt, whanne heuy sleep is wont to occupie men, <b>14</b> drede and tremblyng helde me; and alle my boonys weren aferd. <b>15</b> And whanne the spirit `yede in my presence, the heiris of `my fleisch hadden hidousnesse. 
<b>16</b> Oon stood, whos chere Y knewe not, an ymage bifor myn iyen; and Y herde a vois as of softe wynd. <b>17</b> Whether a man schal be maad iust in comparisoun of God? ethir whethir a man schal be clennere than his Makere? <b>18</b> Lo! thei that seruen hym ben not stidefast; and he findith schrewidnesse in hise aungels. <b>19</b> Hou myche more thei that dwellen in housis of cley, that han an ertheli foundement, schulen be wastyd as of a mouyte. 
<b>20</b> Fro morewtid til to euentid thei schulen be kit doun; and for no man vndurstondith, thei schulen perische with outen ende. <b>21</b> Sotheli thei, that ben residue, schulen be takun awei; thei schulen die, and not in wisdom. 
<b>Job 5</b><br><b>1</b> Therfor clepe thou, if `ony is that schal answere thee, and turne thou to summe of seyntis. <b>2</b> Wrathfulnesse sleeth `a fonned man, and enuye sleeth a litil child. <b>3</b> Y siy a fool with stidefast rote, and Y curside his feirnesse anoon. <b>4</b> Hise sones schulen be maad fer fro helthe, and thei schulen be defoulid in the yate, and `noon schal be that schal delyuere hem. <b>5</b> Whos ripe corn an hungri man schal ete, and an armed man schal rauysche hym, and thei, that thirsten, schulen drynke hise richessis. 
<b>6</b> No thing is doon in erthe with out cause, and sorewe schal not go out of the erthe. <b>7</b> A man is borun to labour, and a brid to fliyt. <b>8</b> Wherfor Y schal biseche the Lord, and Y schal sette my speche to my God. <b>9</b> That makith grete thingis, and that moun not be souyt out, and wondurful thingis with out noumbre. <b>10</b> Which yyueth reyn on the face of erthe, and moistith alle thingis with watris. <b>11</b> Which settith meke men an hiy, and reisith with helthe hem that morenen. 
<b>12</b> Which distrieth the thouytis of yuel willid men, that her hondis moun not fille tho thingis that thei bigunnen. <b>13</b> Which takith cautelouse men in the felnesse `of hem, and distrieth the counsel of schrewis. <b>14</b> Bi dai thei schulen renne in to derknessis, and as in nyyt so thei schulen grope in myddai. <b>15</b> Certis God schal make saaf a nedi man fro the swerd of her mouth, and a pore man fro the hond of the violent, `ethir rauynour. <b>16</b> And hope schal be to a nedi man, but wickidnesse schal drawe togidere his mouth. 
<b>17</b> Blessid is the man, which is chastisid of the Lord; therfor repreue thou not the blamyng of the Lord. <b>18</b> For he woundith, and doith medicyn; he smytith, and hise hondis schulen make hool. <b>19</b> In sixe tribulaciouns he schal delyuere thee, and in the seuenthe tribulacioun yuel schal not touche thee. <b>20</b> In hungur he schal delyuere thee fro deeth, and in batel fro the power of swerd. <b>21</b> Thou schalt be hid fro the scourge of tunge, and thou schalt not drede myseiste, `ethir wretchidnesse, whanne it cometh. 
<b>22</b> In distriyng maad of enemyes and in hungur thou schalt leiye, and thou schalt not drede the beestis of erthe. <b>23</b> But thi couenaunt schal be with the stonys of erthe, and beestis of erthe schulen be pesible to thee. <b>24</b> And thou schalt wite, that thi tabernacle hath pees, and thou visitynge thi fairnesse schalt not do synne. <b>25</b> And thou schalt wite also, that thi seed schal be many fold, and thi generacioun schal be as an erbe of erthe. 
<b>26</b> In abundaunce thou schalt go in to the sepulcre, as an heep of wheete is borun in his tyme. <b>27</b> Lo! this is so, as we han souyt; which thing herd, trete thou in minde. 
<b>Job 6</b><br><b>1</b> Forsothe Joob answeride, and seide, <b>2</b> Y wolde, that my synnes, bi whiche Y `desseruede ire, and the wretchidnesse which Y suffre, weren peisid in a balaunce. <b>3</b> As the grauel of the see, this wretchidnesse schulde appere greuousere; wherfor and my wordis ben ful of sorewe. <b>4</b> For the arowis of the Lord ben in me, the indignacioun of whiche drynkith vp my spirit; and the dredis of the Lord fiyten ayens me. 
<b>5</b> Whether a feeld asse schal rore, whanne he hath gras? Ethir whether an oxe schal lowe, whanne he stondith byfor a `ful cratche? <b>6</b> Ether whethir a thing vnsauery may be etun, which is not maad sauery bi salt? Ether whether ony man may taaste a thing, which tastid bryngith deeth? For whi to an hungri soule, yhe, bittir thingis semen to be swete; tho thingis whiche my soule nolde touche bifore, ben now my meetis for angwisch. <b>8</b> Who yyueth, that myn axyng come; and that God yyue to me that, that Y abide? 
<b>9</b> And he that bigan, al to-breke me; releesse he his hond, and kitte me doun? <b>10</b> And `this be coumfort to me, that he turmente me with sorewe, and spare not, and that Y ayenseie not the wordis of the hooli. <b>11</b> For whi, what is my strengthe, that Y suffre? ethir which is myn ende, that Y do pacientli? <b>12</b> Nethir my strengthe is the strengthe of stoonus, nether my fleisch is of bras. <b>13</b> Lo! noon help is to me in me; also my meyneal frendis `yeden awey fro me. 
<b>14</b> He that takith awei merci fro his frend, forsakith the drede of the Lord. <b>15</b> My britheren passiden me, as a stronde doith, that passith ruschyngli in grete valeis. <b>16</b> Snow schal come on hem, that dreden frost. <b>17</b> In the tyme wherynne thei ben scaterid, thei schulen perische; and as thei ben hoote, thei schulen be vnknyt fro her place. <b>18</b> The pathis of her steppis ben wlappid; thei schulen go in veyn, and schulen perische. <b>19</b> Biholde ye the pathis of Theman, and the weies of Saba; and abide ye a litil. 
<b>20</b> Thei ben schent, for Y hopide; and thei camen `til to me, and thei ben hilid with schame. <b>21</b> Now ye ben comun, and now ye seen my wounde, and dreden. <b>22</b> Whether Y seide, Brynge ye to me, and yiue ye of youre catel to me? ethir, <b>23</b> Delyuere ye me fro the hond of enemy, and rauysche ye me fro the hond of stronge men? <b>24</b> Teche ye me, and Y schal be stille; and if in hap Y vnknew ony thing, teche ye me. <b>25</b> Whi han ye depraued the wordis of trewthe? sithen noon is of you, that may repreue me. 
<b>26</b> Ye maken redi spechis oneli for to blame, and ye bryngen forth wordis in to wynde. <b>27</b> Ye fallen in on a fadirles child, and enforsen to peruerte youre frend. <b>28</b> Netheles fille ye that, that ye han bigunne; yyue ye the eere, and se ye, whether Y lie. <b>29</b> Y biseche, answere ye with out strijf, and speke ye, and deme ye that, that is iust. <b>30</b> And ye schulen not fynde wickidnesse in my tunge, nethir foli schal sowne in my chekis. 
<b>Job 7</b><br><b>1</b> Knyythod is lijf of man on erthe, and his daies ben as the daies of an hired man. <b>2</b> As an hert desireth schadowe, and as an hirede man abideth the ende of his werk; <b>3</b> so and Y hadde voide monethis, and Y noumbrede trauailous niytes to me. <b>4</b> If Y schal slepe, Y schal seie, Whanne schal Y rise? and eft Y schal abide the euentid, and Y schal be fillid with sorewis `til to derknessis. <b>5</b> Mi fleisch is clothid with rot, and filthis of dust; my skyn driede vp, and is drawun togidere. 
<b>6</b> My daies passiden swiftliere thanne a web is kit doun `of a webstere; and tho daies ben wastid with outen ony hope. <b>7</b> God, haue thou mynde, for my lijf is wynde, and myn iye schal not turne ayen, that it se goodis. <b>8</b> Nethir the siyt of man schal biholde me; but thin iyen ben in me, and Y schal not `be in deedli lijf. <b>9</b> As a cloude is wastid, and passith, so he that goith doun to helle, schal not stie; <b>10</b> nether schal turne ayen more in to his hows, and his place schal no more knowe hym. 
<b>11</b> Wherfor and Y schal not spare my mouth; Y schal speke in the tribulacioun of my spirit, Y schal talke togidere with the bitternesse of my soule. <b>12</b> Whether Y am the see, ethir a whal, for thou hast cumpassid me with prisoun? <b>13</b> If Y seie, My bed schal coumfort me, and Y schal be releeuyd, spekynge with me in my bed; <b>14</b> thou schalt make me aferd bi dremys, and thou schalt schake me with `orrour, ethir hidousnesse, `bi siytis. <b>15</b> Wherfor my soule `chees hangyng, and my boonys cheesiden deth. 
<b>16</b> `Y dispeiride, now Y schal no more lyue; Lord, spare thou me, for my daies ben nouyt. <b>17</b> What is a man, for thou `magnifiest hym? ether what settist thou thin herte toward hym? <b>18</b> Thou visitist hym eerly, and sudeynli thou preuest hym. <b>19</b> Hou long sparist thou not me, nether suffrist me, that Y swolowe my spotele? <b>20</b> Y haue synned; A! thou kepere of men, what schal Y do to thee? Whi hast thou set me contrarie to thee, and Y am maad greuouse to my silf? 
<b>21</b> Whi doist thou not awei my sinne, and whi takist thou not awei my wickidnesse? Lo! now Y schal slepe in dust, and if thou sekist me eerli, Y schal not abide. 
<b>Job 8</b><br><b>1</b> Sotheli Baldath Suytes answeride, and seide, <b>2</b> Hou longe schalt thou speke siche thingis? The spirit of the word of thi mouth is manyfold. <b>3</b> Whether God supplauntith, `ethir disseyueth, doom, and whether Almyyti God distrieth that, that is iust? <b>4</b> Yhe, thouy thi sones synneden ayens hym, and he lefte hem in the hond of her wickidnesse; <b>5</b> netheles, if thou risist eerli to God, and bisechist `Almyyti God, if thou goist clene and riytful, 
<b>6</b> anoon he schal wake fulli to thee, and schal make pesible the dwellyng place of thi ryytfulnesse; <b>7</b> in so miche that thi formere thingis weren litil, and that thi laste thingis be multiplied greetli. <b>8</b> For whi, axe thou the formere generacioun, and seke thou diligentli the mynde of fadris. For we ben men of yistirdai, and `kunnen not; for oure daies ben as schadewe on the erthe. <b>10</b> And thei schulen teche thee, thei schulen speke to thee, and of her herte thei schulen bring forth spechis. 
<b>11</b> Whether a rusche may lyue with out moysture? ethir a spier `may wexe with out watir? <b>12</b> Whanne it is yit in the flour, nethir is takun with hond, it wexeth drie bifor alle erbis. <b>13</b> So the weies of alle men, that foryeten God; and the hope of an ypocrite schal perische. <b>14</b> His cowardise schal not plese hym, and his trist schal be as a web of yreyns. <b>15</b> He schal leene, `ether reste, on his hows, and it schal not stonde; he schal vndursette it, and it schal not rise togidere. 
<b>16</b> The rusche semeth moist, bifor that the sunne come; and in the risyng of the sunne the seed therof schal go out. <b>17</b> Rootis therof schulen be maad thicke on an heep of stoonys, and it schal dwelle among stoonys. <b>18</b> If a man drawith it out of `his place, his place schal denye it, and schal seie, Y knowe thee not. <b>19</b> For this is the gladnesse of his weie, that eft othere ruschis springe out of the erthe. <b>20</b> Forsothe God schal not caste a wei a symple man, nethir schal dresse hond to wickid men; 
<b>21</b> til thi mouth be fillid with leiytir, and thi lippis with hertli song. <b>22</b> Thei that haten thee schulen be clothid with schenschip; and the tabernacle of wickid men schal not stonde.
